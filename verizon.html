Overview

Verizon customers struggle to find billing details, make sense of the numbers, and get past the jargon. A team of seven interdisciplinary designers (UX, UI and more) partnered with Verizon's Experience Design team to answer one question: how can AI improve the way customers understand their bill?

Over 16 weeks the team researched the problem across four carriers, designed an AI-powered bill experience, and tested it with 16 people in a Figma prototype. The work moved from open exploration to one focused bet: make the bill explain itself.

The Problem

Verizon bills mix taxes, device payments, and one-time fees, and customers can't tell what changed or why. Confusion leads to frustration and support calls. The AI help that exists today gives generic answers that aren't tied to the bill and sends people to broad FAQ pages, so the charges stay unexplained. Our persona Jason says it bluntly: "If I can't understand my bill, I'll just switch providers."

The Solution

An AI-powered bill experience with four parts:

  • AI Bill Summary: a personalized, plain-language explanation of why the bill changed and who or what caused it.
  • Bill Breakdown: a monthly trend chart, a per-member breakdown, and an FAQ that answers questions like "How can I lower my bill?"
  • Bill PDF with AI: highlighted line items you can click, plus an "Ask me anything about your bill" input.
  • Home shortcut: a balance snapshot with one tap into the breakdown and the AI.

Milestone 1Exploration

The project began with 15+ sources of research, a heuristic evaluation against Nielsen's 10 heuristics, review mining across Verizon, T-Mobile, AT&T, and Mint Mobile, and 7 customer interviews. The evaluation showed that Verizon gives customers almost no visibility into why their bill changes.

Milestone 3Design & testing

Throughout the research phases, the team sketched in short, iterative rounds, working agile-style. Each designer sketched ideas alone, and then the strongest ones were consolidated into a single direction on a whiteboard before building mid-fidelity screens in Figma.

Five screens were tested with 16 participants, using A/B tests and 20–30 minute think-aloud walkthroughs.
