Explaining the bill: AI for billing transparency

"Why is this bill $40 more than last month?" Customers can't get that answer from Verizon today. We designed an AI Bill Summary that explains each change in plain language, and 87.5% of testers were satisfied.

Project manager & UX researcher

Overview

Verizon customers struggle to find billing details, make sense of the numbers, and get past the jargon. Our team of seven interdisciplinary designers (UX, UI and more) partnered with Verizon's Experience Design team to answer one question: how can AI improve the way customers understand their bill?

Over 16 weeks we researched the problem across four carriers, designed an AI-powered bill experience, and tested it with 16 people in a Figma prototype. We moved from open exploration to one focused bet: make the bill explain itself.

The problem

Verizon bills mix taxes, device payments, and one-time fees, and customers can't tell what changed or why. Confusion leads to frustration and support calls. The AI help that exists today gives generic answers that aren't tied to the bill and sends people to broad FAQ pages, so the charges stay unexplained. Our persona Jason says it bluntly: "If I can't understand my bill, I'll just switch providers."

IMAGE → Current Verizon experience: screenshots of the generic AI reply and the FAQ redirect (problem / current-experience slide).

The solution

An AI-powered bill experience with four parts:

  • AI Bill Summary: a personalized, plain-language explanation of why the bill changed and who or what caused it.
  • Bill Breakdown: a monthly trend chart, a per-member breakdown, and an FAQ that answers questions like "How can I lower my bill?"
  • Bill PDF with AI: highlighted line items you can click, plus an "Ask me anything about your bill" input.
  • Home shortcut: a balance snapshot with one tap into the breakdown and the AI.

Process

Milestone 1Exploration

We began with 15+ sources of research, a heuristic evaluation against Nielsen's 10 heuristics, review mining across Verizon, T-Mobile, AT&T, and Mint Mobile, and 7 customer interviews. The evaluation showed that Verizon gives customers almost no visibility into why their bill changes.

Customers feel "surprised" by their bills, and contacting support feels like a hassle.

Our interviews pointed to the same fix: customers want proactive automation and summaries they can trust. That narrowed our scope from "customer service" to billing transparency and trust.

IMAGE → Research synthesis: review-mining pain points and needs, plus heuristic evaluation findings (M1 slides). Keep it to one clear takeaway visual.

Milestone 2Research

We compared six apps (T-Mobile, AT&T, Rocket Money, Xfinity, Mint Mobile, and Google Fi) and ran five cognitive walkthroughs to see where billing breaks down. Two personas anchored our decisions: Sarah, 42, an HR consultant managing a family plan at $265–310 a month, and Jason, 26, a marketing associate paying $85–110 a month.

Sarah's hardest question is "which family member caused the increase?", while Jason just wants a fast answer. Journey mapping showed three problems to solve: billing details are hard to find, the numbers are hard to understand, and the explanations are full of jargon.

IMAGE → Sarah and Jason persona slides (with the "Why is this bill $40 more than last month?" quote), then the journey map / walkthrough pain points.

Milestone 3Design & testing

Throughout the research phases, we sketched in short, iterative rounds, working agile-style. Each of us sketched ideas on our own, and then we consolidated the strongest ones into a single direction on a whiteboard before building mid-fidelity screens in Figma.

IMAGE → Individual sketches, then the merged whiteboard (sketching slides). Caption: what we kept and why.

We tested five screens with 16 participants, using A/B tests and 20–30 minute think-aloud walkthroughs.

Impact & learnings

Results

87.5% of participants (14 of 16) were satisfied, beating our 80% goal.

The AI Bill Summary significantly improved how well people understood their bill, and testers consistently preferred simpler, more visual layouts. Navigation clarity and task flow are the next problems to solve.

What I'd do next: fix the navigation issues, test with real bill data instead of sample numbers, and measure whether the summary reduces support contacts.
