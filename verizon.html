Overview

Verizon customers struggle to find billing details, make sense of the numbers, and get past the jargon. A team of seven interdisciplinary designers (UX, UI and more) partnered with Verizon's Experience Design team to answer one question: how can AI improve the way customers understand their bill?

Over 16 weeks the team researched the problem across four carriers, designed an AI-powered bill experience, and tested it with 16 people in a Figma prototype. The work moved from open exploration to one focused bet: make the bill explain itself.

The Problem

“Why is my bill $50 more than last month?” Sarah, account holder on a family plan

Verizon bills mix taxes, device payments, and one-time fees, and customers can't tell what changed or why. Confusion leads to frustration and support calls. The AI help that exists today gives generic answers that aren't tied to the bill and sends people to broad FAQ pages, so the charges stay unexplained. Our persona Jason says it bluntly: "If I can't understand my bill, I'll just switch providers."

We interviewed 7 Verizon customers about their bills and support. Three themes came up again and again.

  • Billing is a mystery. “Billing is always confusing and often changes without knowing why.”
  • Support can’t explain it either. “People on the phone normally can’t explain why the price is what it is.” Several avoid calling because of long holds.
  • Open to AI, if it’s accurate. One liked the idea of an AI bill breakdown, as long as it’s right.

We read App Store, Reddit and Google reviews for Verizon, T-Mobile, AT&T and Mint. The same complaints came up everywhere: long waits, hard-to-reach humans, and chat that loops.

“This ai customer service is clearly not ready to be used. It doesn’t understand my problems at all and puts me through really frustrating loops”Verizon App Store review
“T-Mobile used to be so good when I call there they always solve problems but past year it looks like they don’t even listen to you…”T-Mobile App Store review

Takeaway: support frustrations are similar across providers, so the best experience limits how much customers need to contact support at all.

We scored Verizon’s billing flow against Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics. These four stood out.

We reviewed 15+ sources on digital customer experience, trust in automated support and how people feel about AI.

So we steered away from a chatbot, kept AI out of high-stress moments, and made it transparent with a human always available.

Narrowed to

Billing transparency and trust

Across interviews, reviews and the heuristic evaluation, the same problem kept coming up: customers can’t tell why their bill changes, and support often can’t explain it either. Customers wanted proactive automation and summaries they could trust.

People don’t want to talk to AI about real issues. Reviews of Verizon, T-Mobile and Mint described chatbots that handle simple tasks, then loop, promise help and leave people hanging, or stand in the way of reaching a human. That told us AI shouldn’t replace support. It should help people understand their bill before they ever need to call.

How might we

Increase customer satisfaction for Verizon's current customers by increasing their knowledge through AI?

This question guided every design decision after it. We aimed for at least 80% positive feedback in testing, and for a bill experience customers trust.

Journey maps of receiving, reviewing and questioning a bill showed the same pattern. Dense layouts, unfamiliar terms and jargon-filled explanations pushed people out of the app and toward support. Their feelings slid from concerned to annoyed to overwhelmed, and finally to losing trust.

"Is this worth calling about?" "It's not even looking at my account."

Key insight

Customers need to know what changed and who caused it, in words they understand. So the bill explains each change in plain language and breaks it down by member.

What the research said about AI and trust

TransparentShow the reasoning

People trust automation more when it explains why, instead of giving generic answers.

Human fallbackAlways an exit

Many worry AI will block them from a person. Easy escalation builds trust.

Right momentsNot a chatbot first

AI is trusted for routine tasks and less in stressful ones, and chatbots read as cold. So the AI explains the bill and leaves the conversation optional.

ProactiveBefore it's a surprise

Highlight changed lines, tag one-time versus recurring charges, and explain increases before customers go looking.

Milestone 3Design & testing

Throughout the research phases, the team sketched in short, iterative rounds, working agile-style. Each designer sketched ideas alone, and then the strongest ones were consolidated into a single direction on a whiteboard before building mid-fidelity screens in Figma.

Five screens were tested with 16 participants, using A/B tests and 20–30 minute think-aloud walkthroughs.

The Solution

An AI-powered bill experience with four parts:

  • AI Bill Summary: a personalized, plain-language explanation of why the bill changed and who or what caused it.
  • Bill Breakdown: a monthly trend chart, a per-member breakdown, and an FAQ that answers questions like "How can I lower my bill?"
  • Bill PDF with AI: highlighted line items you can click, plus an "Ask me anything about your bill" input.
  • Home shortcut: a balance snapshot with one tap into the breakdown and the AI.

02
