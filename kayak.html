Bringing group travel to Kayak

Planning a trip with friends means juggling budget, dates, and location across scattered chats, and Kayak has no group travel features today. I led a team of nine UX designers through research to design them.

Role
Team leader
Team

Timeline

Overview

KAYAK is a travel metasearch engine for flights, hotels, rental cars, and vacation packages. Our goal was to create group travel features for its website for a better desktop experience.

The problem

One person usually leads planning, and price, location, and safety matter most. Group members often search at the same time for the "best deal," so it's hard to compromise on budget, location, and dates, flexibility and privacy suffer, and communication is hard to manage. Today, KAYAK has nothing to support this.

Process

Milestone 1Who are we designing for?

Secondary research showed KAYAK once had a group feature, Trip Huddle, that faded mostly from poor marketing. We then ran 18 interviews with family and friend groups, which showed our primary group-travel users are older Gen Z and Millennials.

Key insight

Groups lean on one organizer and talk mostly by text. So the design gives every member a voice through suggestions and voting, while one admin keeps the final say.

Milestone 2How do groups travel?

To map how groups plan together, I conducted a contextual inquiry with a friend group planning a spring break trip, and our team ran a workshop comparing trip planning on KAYAK and competing sites. Both revealed that planning is non-linear: budget, dates and location are negotiated simultaneously. Location is usually the starting point, but groups change it when the budget does not fit.

Key insight

Budget, dates and location are decided together: people start with location but revise it when the budget does not fit. So the trip overview shows all three as linked tabs, with location first.

Milestone 3How can KAYAK improve?

Before designing, we studied what other platforms had figured out about group travel and where KAYAK fell short. Open each step to see the details.

01Competitive analysisCompetitors had shared calendars, voting and map itineraries that KAYAK lacked.

Each of us researched one competitor, breaking down how it handled budget, location, dates and collaboration.

  • Shared calendar and expenses: everyone adds availability, and it is clear who has paid.
  • Map with activities and a day-by-day itinerary.
  • Voting to settle what the group cannot agree on.
  • Sorting and estimating: Cheapest, Fastest, Recommended, and a budget estimator.
02Heuristic evaluationGroup planning was buried, and hotels could not be saved to a trip.

All nine of us planned a trip on KAYAK as first-time users, four international and five domestic.

1"Create Trip" was tucked in a corner and hard to find.
2The itinerary builder searched by location only, with no dates or times.
3Ads pushed hotels that could not be saved to a trip.
03Feature sketchingFour concepts: a calendar, budget breakdown, voting and a location map.

We sketched fixes for the gaps we found, from group calendars to a map for exploring activities.

Key insight

Competitors had voting and shared expenses, but KAYAK hid even "Create Trip". So collaboration, voting and group budgets became core features, easy to find.

Milestone 4Design and test

We turned these findings into the group travel features below, then ran concept testing. Users preferred the tab overview to a grid, but struggled to tell admins from viewers, so we added clear role indicators.

Key insight

Testers preferred the tab overview but could not tell admins from viewers. So we kept the tabs and added clear role indicators.

The solution

The final designs give a group what it needs on desktop:

  • Change any part of the trip at any time.
  • Suggest and vote. Each member can suggest a destination for the group to vote on.
  • Individual and group budgets. Each person sees their own budget breakdown as well as the group's.

Impact & learnings

My impact

  • Led the team: I led UX designers with varying skill levels through mentorship and collaboration.
  • Led stakeholder meetings to gather feedback and stay aligned.
  • Redesigned core features from user insights.
  • Planned and conducted usability tests.

What I learned

  • Balancing user needs and business goals: prioritizing the changes with the greatest impact on both.
  • Design consistency: keeping patterns and visual language cohesive across features.
  • Agile design: iterating quickly in a fast-paced, cross-functional workflow.

Limitations & next steps

We didn't design for kids or pets, KAYAK is a metasearch with no direct booking, and voting was under-tested. Next I'd test voting on stays, flights and transportation, and test within KAYAK's design system.
