Project overview

KAYAK is a travel metasearch engine for flights, hotels, rental cars, and vacation packages. Our goal was to create group travel features for its website. Today, KAYAK has no group travel features.

My impact

  • Team leader: led a team of UX designers with varying skill levels through mentorship and collaboration.
  • Led stakeholder meetings to gather feedback and stay aligned.
  • Redesigned core features from user insights.
  • Planned and conducted usability tests.

Milestone 1

Identifying users and exploring the journey

Research into travel trends showed one person usually leads planning, price, location, and safety matter most, and group members often search at the same time for the "best deal." Pain points include difficulty compromising on budget, location, and dates, less flexibility and privacy, and hard-to-manage communication.

Our primary group-travel users are older Gen Z and Millennials, based on scholarly research and 30+ interviews.

Milestone 2

Understanding the booking journey

Interviews, a long-term contextual inquiry, and a workshop showed that budget, dates, and location are decided at the same time, and not every traveler wants the same ones. Group members need constant communication.

The most important part of planning a trip is also the most complicated: budget, dates, and location.

Image: journey map

Milestone 3

Pinpointing improvements

Competitive analysis: users should be able to change any part of the trip at any time, each member should be able to suggest a destination for the group to vote on, and each person should see their own budget breakdown as well as the group's.

Heuristic evaluation: "Add to trip" was hard to find, there was no good way to explore activities, the map only appeared after adding something, preferences reset with each category, and every deal opened a new tab.

What I learned

  • Balancing user needs and business goals: prioritizing the changes with the greatest impact on both.
  • Design consistency: keeping patterns and visual language cohesive across features.
  • Agile design: iterating quickly in a fast-paced, cross-functional workflow.
