Hoka · E-commerce · Sponsored project

Helping runners compare shoes without losing their place

Shoppers overlook Hoka's comparison tool, and when they find it, it blocks browsing. Based on workshops with novice and experienced runners and usability testing, I redesigned how customers compare athletic footwear.

Overview

Hoka, a Deckers sub-brand, makes premium performance footwear for athletes of all levels, with a mission to empower a world of athletes to "fly over the earth." Our team researched the current shopping experience to improve how customers compare shoes.

Hoka offers a shoe finder quiz that returns three matches, a side-by-side comparison of up to four shoes, and individual product pages.

The problem

Comfort, price, and style are shoppers' top priorities, but existing tools often recommend random shoes, experienced runners prefer reading product info over quizzes, and size and fit remain a problem. Usability tests of the current site found:

  • Low discoverability: 80% of participants overlooked the comparison tool on first browse.
  • Dialog too large: the bottom compare dialog blocked browsing other products.
  • No explanation of terminology.
  • No option to modify the shoe list.

The solution

I compiled the research into high-fidelity wireframes that help users move through the decision-making process and make an informed selection.

IMAGE → High-fidelity wireframes.

Process

Milestone 1Identifying users and exploring the journey

Previous research, a Reddit deep dive, and Mintel reports showed that comfort, price, and style are the top priorities.

Milestone 2Investigating how users decide

I ran two workshops, each with six participants aged 18–26: one with novice runners and one with experienced runners. Activities included "draw your perfect shoe," ranking, journey mapping, and shoe comparison.

IMAGE → Workshop synthesis.

Milestone 3Building solutions for informed selection

I turned the workshop and usability findings into wireframes, iterating on them through feedback.

Impact & learnings

My impact

  • Created workshops to understand user behavior and refine project goals.
  • Led stakeholder meetings to gather feedback and stay aligned with business goals.
  • Redesigned core features using user insights.
  • Planned and ran usability tests to drive design improvements.

What I learned

  • Collaboration: facilitating workshops and meetings, and aligning different perspectives.
  • User-centered design: iterating on designs through usability testing.
  • Data-driven decisions: synthesizing qualitative and quantitative feedback.
  • Ownership: planning and following through on workshops, documentation, and testing.
