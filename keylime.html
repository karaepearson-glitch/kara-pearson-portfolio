Keylime Interactive · August – December 2024

Increasing financial literacy and promoting healthy financial behaviors with AI

Researching user attitudes and motivations to develop a new AI tool that improves financial behaviors among Gen Z.

Project overview

KLI is a UX research and customer experience service design agency with 15 years of experience and over 150 clients, including Amazon, Roku, Bose, and Google.

The goal: increase financial literacy and promote healthy financial behaviors for college students.

How can we help college students build healthy financial habits, specifically within budgeting?

My impact

  • Accelerated design and research with AI. Led the project end to end, integrating AI into design, research, and documentation workflows — protocol writing, test analysis, early ideation — cutting the timeline nearly in half.
  • Facilitated stakeholder alignment. Owned client communication through feedback sessions and weekly syncs.
  • Mentored the team. Guided teammates in research, ideation, and design.

Milestone 1

Identifying users and exploring the journey

Literature reviews, Reddit analysis, and user interviews revealed patterns of compulsive spending and emotional triggers like guilt, stress, and fear.

  • Students want to budget but feel overwhelmed or don't know how.
  • Budgeting often means "try not to spend money" or "check my account and hope for the best."
  • Many know they should invest but don't know where to start.
  • Financial tools feel too complex or irrelevant.

Findings were synthesized into two personas: Sam, who knows little about finance but is curious and overwhelmed, and Alex, who knows a bit but lacks motivation and trust.

Image: personas

Research revealed not just a skills gap, but a motivation gap.

Milestone 2

Uncovering what's missing

We first aimed to help students start investing. Financial experts told us: "Investing is important — but students need a strong foundation first. Without a budget, they're not ready." That prompted a major pivot to budgeting.

A comparative analysis of Mint, Rocket Money, Acorns, and CLEO, plus three sequential workshops with 30 users, showed that most apps don't fit into students' daily lives and feel boring, complicated, or judgmental.

"I thought I budgeted well… until I looked at my account."

Image: comparative analysis / workshops

Milestone 3

Designing for simplicity, trust, and real use

A co-design workshop produced two ideas: receipt scanning with cashback (already on the market) and an AI budgeting assistant inside an existing banking app — our direction.

  • Students don't want to download another app.
  • They prefer tools that do the work for them.
  • They want real-time feedback, not end-of-month regret.
Image: concept / prototype

Milestone 4

Testing, iterating, and refining

Users found the concept simple and promising, but testing revealed friction: some features were confusing and some moments lacked trust. That feedback led us to re-evaluate how to make the tool clearer, more approachable, and more supportive.
