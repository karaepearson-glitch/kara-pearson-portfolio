Building healthier financial habits for Gen Z

Gen Z wants to budget but finds most tools boring, complicated, or judgmental. I led our team of nine to design an AI budgeting assistant that fits into the banking app students already use, and cut our timeline nearly in half by building AI into our workflow.

Role
Team lead, researcher

Project type
AI budgeting tool for college students

~16 weeks

Overview

Keylime Interactive (KLI) is a UX research and customer experience service design agency with 15 years of experience and 150+ clients, including Amazon, Roku, Bose, and Google. As part of Purdue's Experience Studio, a team of nine worked with three KLI researchers to explore how to build healthy financial habits in college students.

The question: how can we create a solution to help college students build healthy financial habits, specifically within budgeting?

As team lead, I delegated tasks, led sponsor meetings, taught and led team activities, and was the sponsors' point of contact. On the research side I led the literature review, wrote the interview protocol, ran the Reddit dive and competitive analysis, and created and led the workshop and co-design protocols.

The problem

Literature review insights pointed to pressure to spend, compulsive buying, and over-consumption fueled by social media and "buy now, pay later." We interviewed 10 college students (ages 19–22) and 2 financial experts. Students felt overwhelmed, confused, and anxious opening their bank app, and budgeting often meant "just try not to spend money."

"I'm not going to download an app for something that I don't even want to do. I just want it to do it for me."

A competitive analysis showed many tools, each doing different things, and little to no use of AI. Our users were 18–22 year olds who are uninterested in budgeting or lack the knowledge to do it. We captured them in a persona, Sam, a Purdue computer science student with a loan who limits weekly spending but lacks the motivation to use budgeting tools.

IMAGE → Persona: Sam.

The solution

An AI budgeting assistant built into the banking app students already use. Setup is quick because it pulls from their bank data, and the AI builds a plan that gradually builds understanding and habits.

  • Overwhelmed by data: show only relevant data.
  • Lack of knowledge: an easy-to-understand AI plan that explains itself.
  • Lack of motivation, or fear of too much work: bank integration so setup is quick, with a choice between AI and manual input.

Process

Milestone 1Understanding students' financial behaviors

Literature review, competitive analysis, Reddit research, and 30-minute interviews with 10 students and 2 financial experts. One expert noted that college students don't understand that a strong budget is the foundation for financial wellness.

Milestone 2Finding which solutions would help

A 45-minute discovery workshop with 18–22 year olds (three activities, including "What do you budget for?" and "Track your money flow") showed low knowledge, low motivation but a belief that budgeting could matter, and possible motivation from monetary incentives.

A 60-minute co-design workshop with 10 students aged 19–22 uncovered barriers (variable monthly expenses, it takes too long, it's stressful alone, college life) and motivators (avoiding debt, saving for important things, going out with friends, feeling safe at month end). Students wanted features inside their existing bank app that are straightforward and "do it for me." Ideation produced two concepts: an AI budgeting tool and a receipt scanner with cash back.

IMAGE → Co-design workshop and sketches.

Milestone 3Mid-fi designs and validation

We tested both concepts with 12 participants, split nearly 50/50. The receipt scanner was easy to understand, but people rarely save receipts. The AI concept offered clear visuals and both manual and AI options, but trust in AI was lacking. We pivoted to AI: it had the stronger rationale, needed fewer steps, and fit user needs better.

Lo-fi testing with 10 participants showed the "Save $100.00 with our new tool!" banner was confusing (some thought it was a scam) and the pie charts didn't clearly separate current and projected spending.

IMAGE → Lo-fi to mid-fi iteration.

Milestone 4Testing, iterating, and refining

We rewrote the banner in plain language, outlined the new features, and added explanation text. A final trust test with 5 participants found that transparency fosters trust, users want control over what data the AI sees, and AI inside a banking app feels more trustworthy than in a third-party app.

Impact & learnings

My impact

  • Accelerated design and research with AI: I integrated AI into design, research, and documentation, cutting our timeline nearly in half.
  • Aligned stakeholders: I led sponsor meetings and was the main point of contact.
  • Led the team: I delegated work across nine people and led workshops and sketching.

Learnings and limitations

The project was open-ended and research-heavy, which taught me to act on sponsor feedback, evaluate our work critically, and manage time. With more time we would have run more testing and built a hi-fi design. Our team was also part of the user group, which limited our outside knowledge.
