Finding opportunities for innovation in finance

How can digital experiences help people navigate their financial lives? I led a team of nine UX designers through exploratory research, expert interviews, and collaborative workshops to uncover unmet needs and identify opportunities for improvement.

Role
Team leader, UX researcher & designer
Team

Overview

Key Lime Interactive (KLI) is a UX research and customer experience consulting agency. This 16-week sponsored project began with an open-ended challenge: identify opportunities for improvement within the financial sector.

Through exploratory research, interviews, workshops, and concept testing, the project investigated financial behaviors, uncovered unmet needs, and translated findings into a potential digital product.

The Problem

“I know I should be budgeting and investing, but I don’t know where to start.”

Adapted from a student insight in the KLI research

Financial decisions can feel overwhelming, especially for college students navigating financial independence. From everyday spending to saving and investing, understanding how to manage money isn’t always straightforward.

Financial tools offer countless ways to track, manage, and grow money, but knowing which tools to use and where to begin can be difficult.

The challenge was to explore these financial experiences, understand where existing solutions fell short, and identify opportunities to make financial management more approachable.

Process

Milestone 1Understanding the financial landscape

Where are the opportunities for improvement? The project began with exploratory research across budgeting, saving, debt, investing, and financial literacy.

I led the literature review and developed an interview protocol to investigate financial behaviors, knowledge, and challenges.

Research highlighted college students as an audience navigating new financial responsibilities, often without the confidence or knowledge to manage money independently.

Three early findings emerged:

Financial knowledge

Students struggled to understand financial concepts and identify reliable resources.

Spending behaviors

Everyday purchases and social influences made managing money challenging.

Investing

Students expressed interest in growing their money but often lacked the knowledge to get started.

A student persona synthesized the research into a clearer picture of the audience’s needs, motivations, and challenges.

Key insight

College students presented an opportunity to improve financial literacy, with investing and budgeting emerging as areas for deeper exploration.

Milestone 2Finding the right problem

What do students need before they can invest? Early research revealed students’ interest in investing, making it a promising area to explore.

A comparative analysis examined existing financial tools and how they supported financial literacy, budgeting, and investing.

However, interviews with two financial experts challenged the direction. A Purdue financial coach and personal finance professor both emphasized that budgeting was the most important foundation for financial literacy. Students needed to understand their spending and develop healthy financial habits before approaching investing.

The turning point

Before

Helping students get started with investing.

After

Helping students establish the financial foundation needed to invest confidently.

Key insight

Before helping students grow their money, the experience needed to help them understand and manage it.

This redirected the project toward budgeting and the barriers preventing students from developing consistent financial habits.

Milestone 3Understanding budgeting barriers

Why aren’t students budgeting? With budgeting identified as a foundational need, a discovery workshop explored students’ spending behaviors, motivations, and financial challenges.

I created and facilitated the workshop, guiding participants through activities that encouraged reflection on their financial decisions.

Three findings stood out:

Spending awareness

Students frequently spent more than expected, particularly on everyday purchases.

Motivation

Students understood the importance of budgeting but struggled to maintain consistent tracking habits.

Incentives

Rewards generated interest, but participants questioned whether incentives alone would encourage lasting behavior.

Key insight

Students didn’t need convincing that budgeting mattered. They needed an easier way to turn financial awareness into action.

Milestone 4Exploring opportunities

What would students actually use? I designed and facilitated a co-design workshop where students explored potential solutions and identified features that could make budgeting more approachable.

Three priorities emerged:

Automation

Students wanted less manual expense tracking and fewer repetitive tasks.

Accessibility

Simple interactions and understandable information were more appealing than complicated financial features.

Familiarity

Students preferred tools integrated into existing routines, particularly their banking apps.

Key insight

Budgeting could become more accessible through automated experiences that fit into students’ existing financial habits.

Two concepts, one opportunity

Research and ideation informed two potential solutions.

Concept testing with 12 college-aged participants explored both approaches.

Concept 1: Receipt scanner with rewards

A tool that encouraged spending awareness through receipt scanning and financial incentives. Participants appreciated the simplicity and cashback potential but worried about remembering to scan receipts.

Concept 2: AI-assisted budgeting

A tool that could generate spending plans, visualize expenses, and offer automated or manual budgeting. Participants liked the reduced effort and spending visualizations but raised questions about trust and transparency.

Concept testing produced an even split in preferences.

Key insight

Neither concept won outright. However, earlier research highlighted automation and integration into existing banking experiences as promising ways to reduce budgeting barriers.

AI-assisted budgeting became the direction for further design and testing.

Milestone 5Designing and testing

Can AI make budgeting feel easier? The AI-assisted budgeting concept developed into low-fidelity wireframes integrated into a mobile banking app.

Students could generate a spending plan using their financial history or create a budget manually, with visual breakdowns to understand spending patterns.

Testing with 12 college-aged participants explored usability, comprehension, and trust.

12 of 12trusted AI budgeting within a banking app
10 of 12appreciated detailed budget breakdowns
9 of 12wanted more explanation of the features before using AI
6 of 12found aspects of the spending visualizations confusing

Testing also revealed uncertainty around how money would be handled and how some interface elements worked.

Key insight

Students were receptive to AI-assisted budgeting, but transparency and clarity were essential to making the experience understandable.

The Solution

Making budgeting easier through AI

An AI-assisted budgeting experience integrated into a familiar mobile banking app, designed to help college students understand their spending and manage money with less manual effort.

1

Budget where you already bank

Integration into a banking app keeps financial management within an existing routine rather than requiring another platform.

2

Choose AI or manual budgeting

AI-generated spending plans simplify setup, while a manual option gives students control over their financial decisions.

3

Understand spending at a glance

Category breakdowns and spending visualizations help students recognize patterns and better understand their finances.

4

Build confidence through clarity

Feature explanations help students understand automated budgeting and make informed decisions about using AI.

The final mid-fidelity designs bring budgeting into an existing routine, with an emphasis on simplicity, automation, and financial clarity.

The prototype flow

The interactive prototype walks students from the bank home screen through choosing a path, reviewing and editing an AI-generated breakdown, and confirming their plan.

The final mid-fidelity designs focused on reducing effort, improving financial clarity, and making budgeting more approachable for college students.

What I learned

Research can redefine the problem.

Expert interviews challenged the initial interest in investing and revealed a more fundamental opportunity in budgeting.

Understanding behavior matters more than assumptions.

Students recognized the importance of managing money, but motivation and effort prevented consistent action.

Research methods work best together.

Concept testing produced an even split, while earlier findings helped clarify which direction better addressed students’ needs.

Exploratory projects require adaptability.

Leading a team through an undefined problem space reinforced the importance of communication, collaboration, and remaining open to new evidence.
