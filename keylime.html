Building healthier financial habits for Gen Z

Gen Z wants to budget but finds most tools boring, complicated, or judgmental. I led our team to design an AI budgeting assistant that fits into the banking app students already use, and cut our timeline nearly in half by building AI into our workflow.

Overview

KLI is a UX research and customer experience service design agency with 15 years of experience and over 150 clients, including Amazon, Roku, Bose, and Google. They asked us to increase financial literacy and promote healthy financial behaviors for college students.

Our question: how can we help college students build healthy financial habits, specifically within budgeting?

The problem

Literature reviews, Reddit analysis, and user interviews revealed patterns of compulsive spending and emotional triggers like guilt, stress, and fear. Students want to budget but feel overwhelmed or don't know how, and financial tools feel too complex or irrelevant. Budgeting often means "try not to spend money" or "check my account and hope for the best."

Research revealed not just a skills gap, but a motivation gap.

IMAGE → Personas: Sam, who knows little about finance but is curious and overwhelmed, and Alex, who knows a bit but lacks motivation and trust.

The solution

An AI budgeting assistant inside an existing banking app. Our co-design workshop surfaced three things students want, and the concept is built around them:

  • No new app to download. The assistant lives where students already bank.
  • Tools that do the work for them. The AI handles the budgeting so students don't have to.
  • Real-time feedback. Students see how they're doing now, not end-of-month regret.
IMAGE → Concept / prototype screens.

Process

Milestone 1Identifying users and exploring the journey

We combined literature reviews, Reddit analysis, and user interviews, then synthesized the findings into two personas.

Milestone 2Uncovering what's missing

We first aimed to help students start investing. Financial experts told us: "Investing is important, but students need a strong foundation first. Without a budget, they're not ready." That prompted a major pivot to budgeting.

A comparative analysis of Mint, Rocket Money, Acorns, and CLEO, plus three sequential workshops with 30 users, showed that most apps don't fit into students' daily lives and feel boring, complicated, or judgmental.

"I thought I budgeted well… until I looked at my account."

IMAGE → Comparative analysis and workshop synthesis.

Milestone 3Designing for simplicity, trust, and real use

A co-design workshop produced two ideas: receipt scanning with cashback (already on the market) and an AI budgeting assistant inside an existing banking app. We chose the assistant.

Milestone 4Testing, iterating, and refining

Users found the concept simple and promising, but testing revealed friction: some features were confusing and some moments lacked trust. That feedback led us to make the tool clearer, more approachable, and more supportive.

Impact & learnings

My impact

  • Accelerated design and research with AI: I led the project end to end and integrated AI into design, research, and documentation (protocol writing, test analysis, early ideation), cutting the timeline nearly in half.
  • Facilitated stakeholder alignment: I owned client communication through feedback sessions and weekly syncs.
  • Mentored the team: I guided teammates in research, ideation, and design.
